## Browsing and search

- **Home**: the sections you choose, in your order, with Collections and Stations (library radio, your favourites, deep cuts, time travel, a random album), and a quick library switcher.
- **Search**: results as you type. Press Enter (or +) to keep a word as a tag, so "prodigy" then "fire" finds only what matches both. Search for an artist and their best-known tracks come first.
- **One of each**: a song or album you have several copies of shows once, as the best-sounding copy, with its format (FLAC 24/96, MP3 320).
- **Browse** artists, albums, tracks, compilations, soundtracks, collections, genres, styles, moods and decades, with counts from your server. Type in any list to narrow it ("strokes" in Artists), and a matching genre, style or mood is offered as a filter.
- **Formats on album covers**: FLAC 24/96 (gold for high resolution), FLAC 16/44.1, MP3, or Mixed. **Settings › Experience › Show codec information** turns formats off everywhere.
- **Also**: top artists, albums and tracks, Sonic path (a journey between two tracks), and Shuffle library.
- **Shelves** (the rows on Home and on album and artist pages): swipe on a touch screen, or use ‹ › with a mouse. The heading opens the whole list.
- **On any screen**: bottom tabs and a mini player on phones, a sidebar and player bar on computers. Each tab keeps its own Back history, like a phone app. It can be added to a phone's home screen.

## Artist pages

- **Popular tracks**: their best-known songs (Plex's popularity, from Last.fm), each once. Where you have several copies of a song, it plays the original:
  - the studio album's copy where you have it, else a single's, else a compilation's;
  - never a live, remix, demo or session copy, nor one from an album of new recordings of their hits (an orchestral or acoustic re-recording, told by its tracks' lengths), unless that's the only copy you have;
  - of several, the earliest album's, in its best-sounding edition.
- **Their releases by kind**, as Plexamp has them: Albums, Singles & EPs, Live albums, Compilations, Soundtracks, Remixes, DJ mixes, Mixtapes and Demos. The kinds come from Plex.
- **Appears on**: other artists' albums with their tracks on (a compilation, a soundtrack, someone else's album).
- **Appears in**: your playlists and the library's collections with their music in them, saying how many of theirs each has. Smart playlists of the whole library (Plex's All Music) are left out.
- **Similar artists** you have (from Plex), **Sonically similar artists** (from Plex's sonic analysis), their **Styles**, and artist radio.

## Albums, playlists and menus

- **Album and playlist pages**: play, shuffle, a rating on every track, and a menu on every track (play next, add to queue, similar tracks, add to a playlist, appears in, go to the album or artist). Albums show their format and mark their best-known tracks.
- **Playlist radio**: some of the playlist, mixed with tracks that sound like it (or more by its artists, without sonic analysis).
- **Cover menus**: the ⋯ on any album, playlist or collection cover (or a long press): play, shuffle, **Play next**, **Add to queue**, **Add to playlist…**, **Select**, and go to the album or artist.
- **Choosing several at once**: **Select** in a menu, or Ctrl-click (⌘-click on a Mac) or Shift-click on a computer. Ctrl+A or **Select all** chooses everything in the list. What's chosen stays chosen as you search again, so songs from several searches can go into one playlist. The bar at the bottom has **Play next**, **Add to queue** and **Add to playlist** (and for the server's owner, **Add to collection…**).
- **Appears in**, in any track's menu: the albums the song is on, and the playlists and collections it's in.
- **Favourites radio** (Home › Stations): the artists in ❤️ Tracks, their best-known tracks, and artists like them.

## Now Playing and playback

- **Now Playing**: big artwork, a seek bar drawn from your server's loudness analysis, the format (**FLAC 24/96** in gold for high resolution), your rating, Up next, Guest DJ, shuffle, repeat and volume. Add a speed button, a sleep timer and skip buttons in **Settings › Experience › Player**.
- **Converted tracks** (a lower **Streaming quality**, or a format the browser can't play) are marked with their bitrate. Tap the mark to see why. A track already at your quality, or lower, plays as it is.
- **Ratings**: a favourite tick to begin with (five stars in Plex). Stars or thumbs are in **Settings › Experience**. Ratings save to Plex.
- **❤️ Tracks**: tracks you tick, give five stars or a thumbs up go in your ❤️ Tracks playlist, as in Plexamp.
- **Gapless playback and crossfades**: albums play gaplessly. **Smart** crossfade uses your server's loudness analysis to start the next track where the music really ends, over a quiet ending too (**Settings › Playback › Quiet endings** sets how quiet).
- **Loudness levelling** (on to begin with): loud tracks are turned down to match quieter ones, at the level Spotify and TIDAL use. Nothing is turned up, so nothing distorts. Not on iPhone or iPad.
- **Load ahead** (each device's own, one track to begin with): the next 1 to 10 tracks load in full, so a dropped connection doesn't stop the music. Each choice says about how much memory it takes. The track playing always comes first.
- **Keep played tracks** (one to begin with): the last 1 to 5 tracks stay in memory, so going back plays them at once.
- **Skip tracks you've rated down** (on to begin with): a thumbs down, or one star or less, is never picked for you (radio, mixes, shuffles, DJs, AI playlists). What you choose to play plays as it is.
- **Skip live tracks and demos** (off to begin with, on with the MusicBrainz features): leaves them out of what's picked for you. Told by the album's kind in Plex (or MusicBrainz's, for albums Plex hasn't typed), or the title ("(Live)", "(Demo)"). If that leaves too little, they're played.
- **Also**: lock-screen and media-key controls, plays reported to Plex, live updates from other Plex apps (see [Live updates](configuration.md#live-updates)), and album-colour backgrounds.

## Playlists and collections

- **Playlists**: numbered tracks, with an album column on wide screens. Make them, add to them from anywhere, reorder, sort (by artist, track, album or year, with Undo), remove, rename, delete, and change the picture.
- **The lock** keeps a playlist safe from accidental swipes and taps until you unlock it.
- **Collections**, for the server's owner: the same, with tracks, albums or artists.
- Removing a track from a playlist only removes it from the playlist, never from Plex.

## Shared playlists

Plex has no shared music playlists, so the player keeps them: share one with Plex friends who can use your server, and everyone's copy stays the same.

- **Share**: **Share with friends…** in the playlist's menu. Each friend **Can edit** or **Can view**. A mark on the picture shows it's shared.
- **Friends** find it on their Playlists tab under **Shared with you**, and **Add** it or say **No thanks**. Added, it's their own Plex playlist, so it plays in Plexamp too.
- **Kept the same**: changes in plexamp-web reach everyone's copy when they next open it (and every minute while it's open). Changes in other apps go in the next time that person opens plexamp-web.
- **Two people changing it** at once: both changes are kept. Someone who can only view it can't change it: their changes are undone, and they're told.
- **Stopping**: when its owner stops sharing it (or deletes it), everyone keeps their copy as an ordinary playlist. A friend can stop getting it too (**Stop sharing it with me…**).
- **Limits**: not smart playlists, 5,000 tracks at most, and the picture isn't shared. A track a friend can't play is left out of their copy only.
- Everyone needs the same player address, signed in with Plex, and your music library shared with them. The player keeps shared playlists in `/data`.

## Playlist files

- **Export to a file**, in a playlist's menu: a `.playlist.json` with each track's title, artist, album, number, length and Plex ids. Nothing else about your server.
- **Back up your playlists**, in the Playlists tab's ⋯ menu: all of yours in one file (not smart playlists).
- **Import playlists…**, in the same menu: tracks are found by their id, by Plex's id for the recording, by title and artist, or by their number on their album. What isn't found is listed, to copy or save as a spreadsheet (CSV).

Friends who can use your server can import a file you send them and get the same tracks. To keep a playlist the same for both of you, share it instead.

## From Spotify

**Search › From Spotify** makes playlists here from yours on Spotify, using Spotify's download of your data: all your playlists, however long, and your Liked Songs. A free Spotify account works.

1. **Ask for your data**: on spotify.com, **Account › Privacy settings › Download your data**, tick **Account data**, and request it. Spotify emails you when it's ready, usually within a few days.
2. **Choose the files**: open the ZIP and choose only `Playlist1.json` (with `Playlist2.json` and so on, if there are more) and `YourLibrary.json` (your Liked Songs). The rest hold personal details, and the app turns away any other file without opening it.
3. **Choose your playlists**: they're listed with how many tracks each has. One with the name of a playlist you have already comes over as "Name (from Spotify)".
4. **Done**: each track is found in your libraries by its title and artist (a remix or live take only as that very one), and the playlist is made on your Plex server.

- **What's kept from the files**: each playlist's name, and each track's title, artist and album. Everything is read in your browser, and nothing goes to Spotify.
- **What isn't found** is listed (to copy or save as a spreadsheet) and goes on **Missing tracks**, with the Spotify playlist it was in, unless you turn that off for the import.
- **Limits**: 1,000 playlists, 100,000 tracks, 20,000 in one playlist, 25 MB a file. The list says what was cut short.
- `SPOTIFY_FILE_IMPORT=false` on the container turns it off.

### From a link

With `SPOTIFY_LINK_IMPORT=true` on the container (off to begin with), a public playlist comes over straight from its link: **Use a link instead**, under Search › From Spotify.

- **The link**: **Share › Copy link to playlist** in Spotify. The playlist has to be public.
- **Only its first 100 tracks** come over: the player reads the page Spotify shows anyone for the playlist, which lists no more.
- **Nothing about you** goes to Spotify: the player fetches the page, not your browser.
- It's against Spotify's terms, and stops working whenever Spotify changes that page (the app says so).

## Your devices

Signed in to the same Plex account on the same server, your devices share what's playing. Start a playlist on your computer, open the player on your phone, and the phone shows the track, where it's got to and Up next.

- **One device plays**, and the others control it: play, pause, skip, seek, volume and Up next.
- **Listen here**: tap the muted speaker (or **Listen here**), and the music moves to the device in your hand, from the same spot.
- **Carry on later**: close the player, or leave it paused for 10 minutes, and the next device you open has the same queue at the same place.
- **Names**: devices are named like "Chrome on Android". Choose your own in **Settings › Experience › Player name**.
- **Play on two at once**: turn off **Settings › Playback › Share playback with your devices** on one.

Each browser tab counts as a device. Sharing pauses during Play Together.

## Play Together

Listen with Plex friends, each on their own device. Open the **Together** tab, choose **Start a session**, and invite friends you share your Plex library with. They open the same player, sign in with Plex, and find the invitation in their Together tab.

- **In sync**: play, pause, skip and seek happen for everyone, and each track starts at the same moment.
- **One queue**: anyone can add music, remove what's next, shuffle or clear it. Up next shows who added each track.
- **Hosting**: the host invites and removes people. If the host leaves, whoever's been there longest takes over. **End for everyone** stops it.
- **Their own access**: friends play from your server with their own Plex access, so they need your music library shared with them.

Everyone needs the same player address. Guest DJs and repeat are off in a session, and a sleep timer takes you out of the session rather than pausing everyone. It works through work proxies too: it uses ordinary web requests.

## Play on

The cast button (beside Repeat, and in the player bar on computers) chooses where the music plays.

- **Plex players**: Plexamp and other Plex apps that can be controlled remotely, signed in to your account and on the player's network. The app becomes the remote. **This device** brings the music back. For the server's owner, with `PLEX_SERVER_URL` set.
- **AirPlay or Cast…**: the browser's own list of speakers and TVs (AirPlay in Safari, Google Cast in Chrome on Android). Speakers get a temporary token from your Plex server, never your account's. Crossfades are off while one's connected.
- **Alexa (Echo) speakers** can't be played on: Amazon only allows its approved services. Pair one with your phone over Bluetooth instead.

Play on has been tested against a simulated Plex player rather than every real one. If one doesn't respond, please open an issue with the [debug log](configuration.md#debug-mode).

## Guest DJ

Choose a DJ with the DJ button in Now Playing. It plays along with your queue until you turn it off, and then your queue carries on as it was.

| DJ | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| DJ Bridge | Between each of your tracks, one that sits between them in sound. |
| DJ Echo | Follows each of your tracks with the one that sounds most like it. |
| DJ Steady | Keeps the mood going with tracks that sound like the one playing. Your queue waits. |
| DJ Double | Follows each of your tracks with another by the same artist. |
| DJ Era | Keeps the mood going with tracks from the same era. |
| DJ Superfan | Keeps playing the same artist. |
| AI DJ | Your AI model picks each next track and says why (see [ai.md](ai.md)). |
| Queue Echo | Adds nothing: whichever of your next 40 tracks sounds most like the one playing moves up to play next. |
| Queue Bridge | Adds nothing: when your next track sounds far from the one playing, one from later in your queue that sits between them plays first. |

- No DJ adds a song you've played since opening the player (the last 300), or one that's queued. Another version of a song counts as the same song.
- Up next and Now Playing say which tracks a DJ added or moved, and why.
- The sound-based DJs need Plex's sonic analysis; without it, they match on genre.
- They follow **Skip tracks you've rated down** and **Skip live tracks and demos**.

## MusicBrainz features

With `MUSICBRAINZ_ADVANCED=true` (and a `MUSICBRAINZ_URL`: see [configuration.md](configuration.md#musicbrainz)), the player looks your library's artists up on [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org): their other names, genres and every release, with its kind and when it first came out.

- **Nothing else needs them**, and they never change what Plex says your albums are. Each one can be turned off for you in **Settings › MusicBrainz**.
- **What's sent**: artists' names, or the MusicBrainz ids Plex has for them. A mirror of your own is also asked about albums, by their MusicBrainz ids. Album and track titles are never sent: they're compared in the player.
- **What's kept**: MusicBrainz's answers, in `/data`, for a month.
- **How fast**: musicbrainz.org allows one request a second, so a big library takes hours the first time. A mirror of your own is asked up to 8 things at once while it answers within a second (4 to begin with), fewer while its answers take over 2 seconds, and 20 a second at most. A MusicBrainz that says it's busy is left alone for as long as it asks.
- **A mirror without search indexes**: spotted and said so. Albums are still found by the MusicBrainz ids Plex has; the rest wait till its indexes are built.

What they do:

- **Missing albums on artist pages**: **Not in your library**, under an artist's albums, lists the releases you don't have, linked to MusicBrainz, to copy or download (CSV). **Kinds…** chooses which kinds are listed.
- **Original release years**: an artist's albums in the order they first came out, with that year (Plex often has a reissue's year). An album's page says when it first came out.
- **Artists of the same name**: where Plex has two artists of one name as one, the other's albums are kept apart, under **Another artist called …**, with MusicBrainz's description ("Brazilian rapper"). Only on plain evidence: anything less, and an album stays where Plex has it.
- **Other names**: an artist an import can't find by their name is looked for by their other names on MusicBrainz ("Мумий Тролль" and "Mumiy Troll").
- **Skip live tracks and demos**: MusicBrainz's kind counts for albums Plex hasn't typed.

### Years and genres to fix

For the server's owner: **Settings › MusicBrainz › Years and genres to fix**.

- **What it finds**: albums whose year in Plex is later than when they first came out (often a reissue's), and albums without a genre that MusicBrainz has genres for.
- **Exact matches only**: the release Plex has the album as (by its MusicBrainz id), or a studio album of the very same title. Singles, EPs, compilations and live albums are only matched by Plex's id.
- **Checked by their tracks**, with a mirror of your own: the year is that of the first release with the album's very tracks (by their titles and lengths), so a re-recording or remix package never gets an earlier year.
- **Fixing**: **Use 1977** and **Add** (or **Fix all** and **Add all**) change the album in Plex, locked so Plex's refresh leaves it, with **Undo** just after. Genres use your library's own names ("Hip-Hop").
- **The check**: it reads your albums, then their artists, then checks years and genres together. The lists grow as it goes, with a spinner beside each count, and **Fix all** and **Add all** appear once it's done. During a Fix all, the count in the list's heading goes down as each album is fixed.
- **Leaving**: it keeps going while the app's open, on any page. Close it and it stops. Next time, in any browser, it catches up quickly with what the player already knows (answers are kept a month), then carries on. **Look again** reads your library afresh.
- **Your files**: Plex never writes to them, so download the list (CSV, with each album's MusicBrainz id) for [MusicBrainz Picard](https://picard.musicbrainz.org) or [beets](https://beets.io).

### Artists to merge

For the server's owner: **Settings › MusicBrainz › Artists to merge**. For your information only: nothing is changed.

- **One artist, more than once**: Plex links them to the same artist on MusicBrainz. Merge them in Plex.
- **Can't tell**: Plex hasn't linked them all.
- **Different artists, same name**: Plex links them to different artists, usually rightly. Nothing to do, unless one has the other's albums (then **Fix Match** in Plex).
- **Several artists in one name** ("Alphabeat;FORCE"): the files' album artist tag joins several with `;`. Fix the tag, or merge it in Plex.

Each is listed with how many albums it has and who MusicBrainz says it is. To merge in Plex Web, select the artists, then **Merge**.

## Settings in the app

- **They follow your Plex account**: any browser you sign in on with the same player has them, Home layout included. The change made last wins. Turn this off for your account in **Settings › Account › On all your devices** (or for everyone with `SYNC_SETTINGS=false`).
- **Each device's own**: Streaming quality, the route to your server, the player name, sharing playback with your devices, playback speed, Load ahead, and your own AI server.
- **Settings › Experience**, laid out like Plexamp's: appearance, the player, the Home screen, grid and font size, the rating system, codec information, popular-track badges, preview buttons, 1-tap playback, recent plays, desktop integration (media keys, the tab title, keyboard shortcuts) and the player name.
- **Settings › Playback**: streaming quality, gapless, crossfades, Load ahead, Keep played tracks, loudness levelling, what to skip, and sharing playback with your devices.
- **Settings › Connection**: the route to your server, and how live updates arrive (see [configuration.md](configuration.md#reaching-your-plex-server)).

### Switching user, in a Plex Home

Where your account is in a Plex Home, **Settings › Account › Switch user** lists everyone in it, and asks for a PIN where they have one.

- **The Home's admin and the server's owner** are only offered if they have a PIN, so nobody else can get into their account with a tap.
- **Each person is their own**: your settings are kept aside on the device for when you switch back, and the next person gets theirs. The queue, searches and anything else kept for you go, as on signing out.
- **The PIN** goes to plex.tv only.
- **Their own access**: playlists, ratings and plays are theirs in Plex. They stay on the same server and library if they can use it, or choose one.

## Good to know

- **Formats**: files play as they are where the browser can (FLAC, MP3, AAC, Opus, WAV; ALAC mostly in Safari). Anything else is converted by your server.
- **Sonic features** (similar tracks, Sonic path, sonically similar artists, artist radio, the sound-based DJs) need sonic analysis turned on for the library in Plex.
- **Smart crossfade and the waveform** need Plex's loudness analysis (**Settings › Library › Analyze audio tracks for loudness** in Plex). Without it, Smart fades at the very end of each track.
- **iPhone and iPad** don't let web pages change the volume, so there's no crossfade or loudness levelling there.
- **When the connection drops**: what's loaded keeps playing, and a track that can't load waits for the connection rather than being skipped. It's never switched to another quality for you: the player suggests a lower Streaming quality instead. Plays and ratings made meanwhile reach Plex once it's back.
- **Without the internet**: with your Plex server letting the player in, everyone signed in carries on, and an offline password can sign in afresh (see [Offline mode](configuration.md#offline-mode)).
- **Memory**: long lists keep artwork only near the screen, and pages you've left are let go after a while, so the player stays light however big the library.
- **Not built yet**: lyrics.
