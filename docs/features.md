## Browsing and search

- **Home**: sections you choose and order (one layout for every library and server: see [Settings in the app](#settings-in-the-app)), including Collections and Stations (library radio, your favourites, deep cuts, time travel and a random album), and a quick library switcher.
- **Search**: live results, narrowed word by word: press Enter (or +) to keep a word as a tag, and what you type next only finds things that match every tag ("prodigy", then "fire"). One tap on × clears it all. Search for an artist ("groove armada") and Tracks starts with their best-known ones. Each song and album shows once, however many copies your library has (an album and its hi-res edition, a song and its best-of), as the best-sounding copy, with its format (FLAC 24/96, MP3 320).
- **Browsing**: artists, albums, tracks, compilations, soundtracks, collections, genres, styles, moods and decades, with counts from your server. The counts are kept in the browser (just the numbers, until you sign out), so the lists show at once, in their places, and are brought up to date as your server answers: nothing moves while you're looking (a list your library has stopped or started having goes or comes the next time). Any list can be searched within: what you type narrows it as you type ("strokes" in Artists shows The Strokes), and a matching genre, style or mood is offered too (in the 1990s, type "rock" for rock albums from the 1990s, then "calm" for a mood; take each filter off again with a tap). Also top artists, albums and tracks, Sonic path (a journey between two tracks) and Shuffle library.
- **Artist pages**: popular tracks; all their releases by kind, as Plexamp has them: **Albums**, then **Singles & EPs**, Live albums, Compilations, Soundtracks, Remixes, DJ mixes, Mixtapes and Demos, all from Plex, and nothing else (MusicBrainz never changes which kind one is). Plex keeps an artist's albums proper (its "true albums") apart from the rest of their releases, so the app asks for both: the library's albums by them, and which of those are albums proper, then the rows Plex has for the artist's page (which Plexamp shows), and, for any they don't place, the album's own release types (40 albums to a request); one Plex keeps apart but says nothing more of is under Other. **Appears on**: other artists' albums with tracks of theirs on (a compilation, a soundtrack, someone else's album they're featured on), as Plex's row for it has them, or else as Plex's search finds them; **Appears in**: your playlists and the library's collections with their music in them (their tracks, or tracks credited to them, as on a compilation; their albums; them), each saying how many of theirs it has, playlists first, each by name (not a smart playlist of the whole library, such as Plex's All Music, which every track is in). Plex can't be asked which playlists hold an artist's music, so each is looked through, as no more than its tracks' ids and whose they are, once the page comes near the section; what was found is kept until a playlist changes (and used by a track's Appears in too), so the next artist's page is quick. **Similar artists** that your library has (as Plex says, from its metadata) and **Sonically similar artists** (from Plex's sonic analysis), each a shelf with a page of more (›); **Styles**, each opening your library's artists of that style; and artist radio.
- **Album and playlist pages**: play, shuffle, a rating on every track (a favourite tick, stars or thumbs, as you choose), and a menu on every track (play next, add to queue, similar tracks, add to a playlist, appears in, go to album or artist). Albums show their format (FLAC 24/96, say) and mark their best-known tracks. Playlists have **Radio** too: some of the playlist, mixed with tracks that sound like it (or more from its artists, without sonic analysis).
- **Formats on album covers**: as you browse (an artist's albums, the album lists, Home, collections), each album's format is over its cover, as on its page: **FLAC 24/96** in gold for high resolution, **FLAC 16/44.1**, **MP3**, or **Mixed** for an album of several formats. Albums in search have theirs before the artist. So an album and its hi-res edition, or a remaster, can be told apart at a glance. Your server is asked about each album once it's been in view for a moment, a few at a time, and what it says is kept while the app's open (looked up again an hour later, or after a scan of the library); **Settings › Experience › Show codec information** turns them off, with every other format and quality the app shows (on album pages, in search and in the player): one switch for them all, from 0.25.0.
- **A menu on every album, playlist and collection cover**: the ⋯ in the corner of its artwork (with the play buttons, when the pointer's over it), or a long press on a touch screen; albums in search have a ⋯ of their own. An album's has play, shuffle, **Play next**, **Add to queue**, **Add to playlist…**, **Select**, and go to the album or artist.
- **Choosing several at once**: **Select** in a track's or album's menu, or Ctrl-click (⌘-click on a Mac) or Shift-click on a computer (on a cover, instead of opening it in a new tab or window). While choosing, a tap on a track or album chooses it or lets it go again, Shift-click chooses everything from the one chosen last, and Ctrl+A (or **Select all**) everything loaded in that list. What's chosen stays chosen as you search again, sort the list another way or take a filter off, so songs from several searches can go into one playlist. The bar at the bottom says how many are chosen and has **Play next**, **Add to queue** and **Add to playlist** for them all (albums by their tracks: those on the page in the order they're shown, the rest in the order they were chosen), and in its ⋯, play, shuffle and (for the server's admin, of one kind) **Add to collection…**. Doing one of those lets go of what it was done with, once it's in (what's chosen meanwhile stays chosen); ✕, Esc or going to another page stops. Not for Up next itself, or a playlist that's unlocked for editing.
- **Favourites radio** (Home › Stations): the artists in ❤️ Tracks, those you love most first, some of their best-known tracks, and artists like them.
- **Appears in**, from any track's menu: the albums the song is on (its own, and others with the same song by the same artist, such as a compilation, a soundtrack or a live album), the playlists it's in, and the collections it's in, including collections of its album or its artist. Plex says which collections the track, its album and its artist are in, so those come at once (smart collections, which follow their rules, are looked through); Plex can't be asked which playlists hold a track, so each of your playlists is looked through, as no more than its tracks' ids (and whose they are, for an artist's Appears in), and what was found is kept until the playlist changes. A smart playlist whose rules are the whole library (Plex's All Music, say) isn't listed, or read: every track is in it. An artist's page has an **Appears in** of its own: see Artist pages.
- **Responsive**: bottom tabs and a mini player on phones; a sidebar and full transport bar on computers. On a big screen, pages to browse use the whole width, with bigger artwork as the window grows, and settings, Play Together and the AI playlist keep to a column in the middle. Each tab keeps its own history, like a phone app's, so Back stays within the tab you're in. It can be added to a phone's home screen.
- **Shelves** (the rows on Home and album and artist pages) are swiped on a touch screen, where the heading opens the whole list. With a mouse, ‹ › by the heading scroll the shelf a screenful at a time (‹ once it's moved, › until the end), and the title opens the whole list.

## Now Playing and playback

- **Now Playing**: big artwork, a seek bar drawn from your server's loudness analysis, the file's format as a badge (**FLAC 24/96** in gold for high resolution, **MP3 320**) with your rating beside it (it saves to Plex), Up next (with **Move to top**), Guest DJ, shuffle, repeat and volume. Playback speed, a sleep timer and quick skip buttons can be added in **Settings › Experience › Player**.
- **Converted tracks** (a lower **Streaming quality** in Settings › Playback, or a format the browser can't play) are marked by their bitrate, after the file's format (which is dimmed then): hover over the mark, or tap it, to see why. A track already at the quality you chose, or lower, plays as it is.
- **Skip live tracks and demos** (**Settings › Playback**): leaves them out of what the app picks for you, so radio and stations, shuffling an artist or the library, and the Guest DJs keep to studio recordings. A track is live or a demo by its album's kind in Plex (a live album, a demo), or, with the [MusicBrainz features](#musicbrainz-features), MusicBrainz's kind for an album Plex hasn't one for, or by its title ("(Live)", "- Live at…", "(Demo)", "Rough Mix"). An album or playlist you play yourself plays as it is, and where leaving them out would leave too little to play (an artist with nothing but live albums, a radio with hardly anything else), they're played. Off to begin with; on to begin with where the player has the MusicBrainz features, as it was before 0.28.0, when it was one of them.
- **Skip tracks you've rated down** (**Settings › Playback**, on to begin with): a track you've given a thumbs down, or one star or less (Plex's 2 of 10, or less), here or in any Plex app, is never picked for you: not for artist, playlist and favourites radio, Home's mixes and stations (Random album too), shuffling an artist or the library, similar tracks, Sonic path, the Guest DJs or AI playlists. An album or playlist you play yourself plays as it is (shuffled too), and so does a track you choose. Ratings are each Plex account's own, so in a Plex Home what one of you rates down is left out for them alone. Two stars isn't rated down.
- **Loudness levelling** (**Settings › Playback**, on to start with): louder tracks are turned down to match quieter ones, from your server's loudness analysis, to the level Spotify and TIDAL play at. Nothing is ever turned up, so nothing distorts, and no preamp or limiter is needed. Albums played in order keep their own ups and downs. Not on iPhone or iPad, which don't let a web page set the volume.
- **Load ahead** (**Settings › Playback**, each device's own, the next track to start with): the next 1 to 10 tracks loaded in full, so a dropped or slow connection doesn't stop the music at the next track. Each choice says about how much memory it takes (four-minute tracks: about 10 MB each as MP3, 30 MB as CD-quality FLAC, or at the bitrate they're converted to). The track playing comes first: tracks load ahead only while it has what it needs, and wait whenever it needs more. Until it's all loaded, loading ahead is held back too, in small parts with a rest after each, so it takes half the connection at most and leaves the track playing at least twice its bitrate (on a connection too slow for that, a small part every half a minute); once it's all in, the next tracks load as fast as the connection allows. The browser keeps them only while they're wanted (with the tracks played it keeps, as much as the device's memory allows where the browser says how much it has, as Chrome and Edge do over HTTPS: 128 MB to 512 MB; otherwise 384 MB, or 256 MB on a phone or tablet): the app doesn't save them and they don't go in the browser's cache, though a browser short of memory can set large files aside in its own temporary storage until they're let go. Converted tracks load ahead in browsers that play them through the app's stream player (not Safari, which streams them as they play). Off, the next track starts loading shortly before it's due (for a gapless change or a crossfade), once the one playing has loaded.
- **Keep played tracks** (**Settings › Playback**, each device's own, 1 to start with): the last 1 to 5 tracks played stay in memory, so going back to one (Previous, or a track in the queue) plays it at once, even with a slow or dropped connection; the one gone back from is kept too. What was loaded ahead for a track is kept as it is (a file still loading when it starts playing goes on loading); a converted track that came in as it played is kept as it came in (no second stream of it); a file that came in as it played is read again into memory once the tracks ahead are in, with Load ahead on (off, it isn't read twice, so going back to it plays it from your server again). Each choice says about how much memory it takes, and the tracks ahead come first: those played make room for them, the furthest back first, and one still being read waits, part-read, while a track ahead loads. A track played again from memory is another playback, as far as Plex is concerned. Off, a track played again comes from your server again.
- **Gapless playback and crossfades** like Plexamp's. **Smart** crossfade uses your server's loudness analysis to fade into the next track where the music really ends, so silence at the end of a track doesn't leave a gap, and a quiet ending (a beat ticking on under a fade, a hushed outro) doesn't hold the next track up (**Settings › Playback › Quiet endings** sets how quiet). Over a quiet ending the fade has its own length: it lasts as long as the ending takes to go nearly silent, so the next track reaches full volume just as this one fades away (at least 2 seconds, at most 15); a track that ends cold fades for **Crossfade length**. Tracks that follow each other on an album play gaplessly.
- **Ratings**: a favourite tick to start with (a ring beside each track and in Now Playing; ticked, it turns green), which is five stars in Plex. Five stars and thumbs are in **Settings › Experience** (a thumbs up is five stars, a thumbs down one star; with thumbs, a track another Plex app gave two stars or so has neither). A track rated down isn't picked for you: see Skip tracks you've rated down.
- **❤️ Tracks**: tracks you tick, give five stars or a thumbs up go in your ❤️ Tracks playlist, and come out when they lose that rating, as in Plexamp.
- **Lock-screen and media-key controls**, and play reporting to Plex (play counts, recent plays, its dashboard).
- **Live updates**: new music, playlist changes and ratings from other Plex apps appear without reloading (see [configuration.md](configuration.md#live-updates)).
- **Album-colour backgrounds** from Plex's artwork colours, or a blurred copy of the cover.

## Playlists and collections

- **Playlists**: numbered tracks, with each one's album in a column of its own where the screen is wide enough. Make them, add to them from any track, album, artist or the queue, and edit them: reorder, sort (by artist, track name, album or year, or reversed, with Undo), remove, rename, delete, and change the picture (unlocked, a pencil over the artwork uploads one from the device, goes back to one it had, or to the covers Plex puts together). A lock keeps them safe from accidental swipes and taps until you unlock one.
- **Collections**, for the server's owner: the same editing, sorting and pictures, and adding tracks, albums or artists.

## Shared playlists

Plex has no shared music playlists, so the player does it: share a playlist with Plex friends who can use your server, and everyone's copy of it stays the same.

- **Share** from the playlist's menu: **Share with friends…** lists your Plex friends, and for each you choose **Can edit** (their changes go to everyone) or **Can view** (they can play it, not change it). Friends who can't play from your server are shown, but can't be chosen. A mark at the top left of the playlist's picture shows it's shared, and the line under its title says with whom; tap it to change who, or to **Stop sharing**.
- **Your friends** find it on their Playlists tab, under **Shared with you** (a dot on the tab says something's there), with **Add** or **No thanks**. Added, it's a playlist of their own in Plex, so it plays in Plexamp and every other Plex app too.
- **Kept the same.** A change made in plexamp-web goes into the shared playlist straight away, and into each person's copy when their plexamp-web brings it up to date: when they open it, when they open the playlist, and every minute while it's open. Changes made in another app, such as Plexamp, go in the next time that person opens plexamp-web. Renaming it is up to whoever shared it.
- **When two people change it** before either opens plexamp-web again, both changes are kept: tracks added on either side are in, tracks taken out on either side are out. Someone who can only view it can't change it: their changes are undone, and they're told.
- **Stopping.** When whoever shared it stops sharing it, or deletes it, everyone keeps their copy as an ordinary playlist, without the mark. A friend can stop getting it too (**Stop sharing it with me…** in its menu).

Everyone uses the same player address, signed in with Plex, and the friend needs your music library shared with them in Plex. Each person's own browser changes their own copy, so nobody's sign-in is shared. The player keeps what's shared in `/data`: give the container a volume there. Smart playlists can't be shared, a shared playlist holds up to 5,000 tracks, and its picture isn't shared. A track a friend can't play (from a library you haven't shared with them) is left out of their copy, and stays in everyone else's.

## Playlist files

- **Export a playlist** from its menu: **Export to a file** saves a `.playlist.json` file with each track's title, artist, album, number, length and Plex's ids for it, and nothing else about your server.
- **Back up all of yours** from the Playlists tab's ⋯ menu: **Back up your playlists** saves every playlist of yours on the server in one file (not smart ones: their rules belong to the server).
- **Import** from the same menu: **Import playlists…** opens a file. One playlist asks for its name; a backup asks which to bring back, leaving out those you have already. Each track is looked for in every music library on the server, the one you're in first: by its id on the same server, by Plex's id for the recording on a server that's been set up again, by title and artist anywhere else, and last on its album by its number there. What isn't found is listed with its album, year, number and length, to copy or save as a spreadsheet (CSV).

Friends with access to your server can import a file you send them, and get the same tracks. To keep a playlist the same for both of you, share it instead.

## From Spotify

**Search › From Spotify** makes playlists here from your Spotify ones, using Spotify's own download of your data: all your playlists, however long, and your Liked Songs. It works with a free Spotify account, and is turned on with `SPOTIFY_FILE_IMPORT=true` on the container (it's off unless you turn it on, for now: see [configuration.md](configuration.md#container-settings)).

- **Why a download**: Spotify only lets other apps read your playlists through its developer programme, which needs a Premium account and allows five people (only a business with 250,000 users can have more). But anyone can download their own data from Spotify.
- **Asking for it**: on spotify.com, open your account's **Privacy settings**, tick **Account data** under **Download your data**, and request it. Spotify emails you to confirm, then again when it's ready, usually within a few days (it allows itself up to 30). The guide in the app links to the page.
- **Which files**: it comes as a ZIP, with a file for each kind of data. Only these are needed: `Playlist1.json` (with `Playlist2.json` and so on, if you have a lot of playlists), with your playlists, and `YourLibrary.json`, with your Liked Songs. Open the ZIP and choose just those (or drop them on the guide): the other files hold your personal details (your email, date of birth, address and phone number, your payment card, and what you've searched for and listened to).
- **What's read**: only files with those names are opened, in your browser. Any other file you choose, the ZIP itself included, is turned away by its name without being opened, and the app says which. From the files it opens, only each playlist's name and each track's title, artist and album are kept. Descriptions, follower counts, dates and Spotify's ids are left behind, and so are podcast episodes and audiobooks. Files of your own you'd added to Spotify come over by their artist, album and title.
- **Choosing**: your playlists are listed, Liked Songs first, with how many tracks each has. Those with the name of a playlist you have already aren't chosen to begin with; chosen, one comes over as "Name (from Spotify)" (or "(from Spotify 2)", and so on), and so does the second of two by the same name, as the list shows before you start. At most 1,000 playlists and 100,000 tracks in all are read at once, and 20,000 tracks of any one playlist: the list says what was cut short, and when choosing a file on its own would bring over the rest. A file over 25 MB is passed over, saying so.
- **Finding them**: each track is looked for in every music library on the server, the one you're in first, by its title and artist, its album choosing between copies. Spotify's extras on a title ("Song - Remastered 2011", "(feat. …)") don't get in the way. Those found make a playlist in the same order.
- **What isn't found** is listed when it's done, to copy or save as a spreadsheet, and goes on **Missing tracks** (Settings › AI › Missing tracks), saying which Spotify playlist it was in (and how many of yours it's in, up to "20+", each once however often it's imported), unless you turn that off for the import. Missing tracks keeps the latest 1,000 from Spotify for each server, apart from the 500 AI playlists suggested, so a whole account's can't push those out: past that, the import's spreadsheet has them all, and it says so. It's kept for your Plex account, and synced into the Plex server owner's Missing tracks: when a friend imports their playlists, what your server doesn't have is on your Missing tracks too, with their name, but not their playlists' names (see [ai.md](ai.md#on-every-device-and-for-the-servers-owner)). With `SYNC_MISSING_TRACKS=false`, it's on the device that did the import, for them to share with you.
- **What goes where**: nothing goes to Spotify, and to the container's service only what isn't found, for Missing tracks kept for your account (each track's title and artist, and a short hash of the playlist's name, too short to work the name out from, never the name), unless you turn that off for the import: on the container's `/pms/` route to your server, its sign-in check is given only your token, not what's asked for. The titles, artists and albums go no further than your Plex server, to find the tracks there (as any search does), and the playlists you choose are made there.

### From a link

With `SPOTIFY_LINK_IMPORT=true` on the container (it's off unless you turn it on: see [configuration.md](configuration.md#container-settings)), one public playlist comes over straight away, from its link, but only its first 100 tracks: **Use a link instead** in the same place, or, with only this way on, straight from **Search › From Spotify**.

- **The link**: in Spotify, open the playlist and choose **Share › Copy link to playlist** (an `open.spotify.com/playlist/…` link, a `spotify:playlist:…` URI, or a `spotify.link` short link). The playlist has to be public.
- **What's read**: its name, who made it, and each track's title, artists and length, from the page Spotify shows anyone for the playlist. That page lists the first 100 tracks at most, so a longer playlist comes over as its first 100. Podcast episodes in it are left out.
- **Finding them**: as above, with its length choosing between copies too, and a list of artists ("Artist, Guest") not getting in the way. The playlist is named as on Spotify (or with "(from Spotify)" when you have one of that name), which you can change first. What isn't found goes on Missing tracks, as above.
- **What goes where**: the link goes to the container, which reads the page from Spotify. Nothing goes from your browser to Spotify, and nothing about you or your library goes to Spotify.

Spotify's page isn't an API: when Spotify changes it, this stops working until plexamp-web is updated, and the app says so.

## Your devices

Signed in to the same Plex account and playing from the same server, your devices share what's playing. Start a playlist on your computer, open the player on your phone, and the phone shows the track, where it's got to and Up next, with its own sound off.

- **One device plays.** The others show where the music is ("Playing on Firefox on Windows") and control it: play, pause, skip, seek, volume, shuffle, repeat and changes to Up next all happen there. Paused from another device, the one playing says so ("Paused from Chrome on Android"); a play, pause, skip or seek that took more than 30 seconds to get there (that device out of touch meanwhile) is let go. Another device takes over once it's playing, not as it starts to.
- **Listen here.** Tap the muted speaker (or **Listen here** under the artwork) and the music moves to the device in your hand, from the same spot. Browsers only start sound after a tap on the device itself.
- **Carry on later.** Close the player where it was playing, or leave it paused for 10 minutes, and the next device you open has the same queue ready at the same place.
- **Name your devices** in **Settings › Experience › Player name**. Until you do, they're named by browser and system, like "Chrome on Android".
- **Play on more than one at once**: turn off **Settings › Playback › Share playback with your devices** on a device, and it plays on its own.

Each browser tab counts as a device. While you're in a Play Together session, sharing between your devices pauses.

## Play Together

Listen with Plex friends, each on their own device. Open the **Together** tab, choose **Start a session**, and pick friends to invite (people you share your Plex library with). They open the same player, sign in with their own Plex account, and find the invitation in their Together tab.

- **In sync**: play, pause, skip and seek happen for everyone, and each track starts at the same moment for everyone.
- **One shared queue**: anyone in the session can add music from anywhere in the app, remove upcoming tracks, shuffle or clear what's next. Up next shows who added each track.
- **Hosting**: the host can invite more friends and remove people. If the host leaves, whoever's been there longest takes over. **End for everyone** stops the session.
- **Your own server access**: friends play from your Plex server with their own access, so they need your music library shared with them. Plays count for each person.

Everyone must use the same player address, because sessions live in that container. Guest DJ and repeat are off during a session, and a sleep timer takes you out of the session instead of pausing everyone. Sessions use plain HTTP requests, so they work through work proxies too.

## Play on

The cast button next to Repeat (and in the desktop player bar) chooses where the music plays.

- **Plex players**: Plexamp (on a Raspberry Pi or a computer, say) and other Plex apps that can be controlled remotely, signed in to your Plex account and on the same network as the container. The app becomes the remote, and Now Playing follows what the player plays. **This device** brings the music back, from where it got to. This is for the server's owner, and needs `PLEX_SERVER_URL` set (see [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md#play-on)).
- **AirPlay or Cast…** opens the browser's own list of speakers and TVs, where it has one: AirPlay in Safari, Google Cast in Chrome on Android. The speaker gets a temporary token from your Plex server, never your account's own. Crossfades are off while a speaker is connected.
- **Alexa (Echo) speakers can't be played on**: Amazon only lets its approved music services play on them. Pair the speaker with your phone or computer over Bluetooth instead.

Plex doesn't fully document how its players are controlled, and Play on has been tested against a simulated player rather than every real one. If a player doesn't respond, please open an issue, with the [debug log](configuration.md#debug-mode).

## Guest DJ

Choose a DJ with the DJ button in Now Playing. It plays along with your queue until you turn it off, and then what's to come is as it was.

| DJ | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| DJ Bridge | Between each of your tracks and the next, plays one that sits between them in sound. |
| DJ Echo | Follows each of your tracks with the one that sounds most like it. |
| DJ Steady | Keeps the mood going with tracks that sound like the one playing. Your queue waits until you turn it off. |
| DJ Double | Follows each of your tracks with another by the same artist. |
| DJ Era | Keeps the mood going with tracks from the same era. |
| DJ Superfan | Keeps queueing tracks by the same artist. |
| AI DJ | Your AI model picks each next track from your library and says why (see [ai.md](ai.md)). |
| Queue Echo | Adds nothing. As each track starts, whichever of your next 40 sounds most like it moves up to play next. |
| Queue Bridge | Adds nothing. When your next track sounds far from the one playing, one from later in your queue that sits between them plays first. |

No DJ adds a song you've played since opening the player (the last 300), or one that's queued, and another version of a song (on a compilation, a live album or a remaster) counts as the same song. Up next and Now Playing say which tracks a DJ added or moved, and why. The sound-based DJs need Plex's sonic analysis (see [Good to know](#good-to-know)); without it, the ones that add tracks match on genre. They never add a track you've rated down (unless **Skip tracks you've rated down** is off), and with **Skip live tracks and demos** they leave live tracks and demos out (see [Now Playing and playback](#now-playing-and-playback)).

## MusicBrainz features

With `MUSICBRAINZ_ADVANCED=true` on the container (and a `MUSICBRAINZ_URL`: see [configuration.md](configuration.md#container-settings)), the player looks your library's artists up on [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org): their other names, their genres, and their release groups (every album, EP and single, with its kinds and when it first came out). It asks by artist, so an album needs nothing more but its genres, and keeps what MusicBrainz says for a month, in `/data`. Only names go to MusicBrainz: an artist's (an album's title is matched in the player). On musicbrainz.org it asks once a second at most, its rule, so the first look at a big library takes a while; a mirror of your own answers as fast as it can, and keeps the names on your network. Each one can be turned off for you in **Settings › MusicBrainz**. None of them is needed for anything else, and none changes what the app shows of your library as Plex has it: an artist's Singles & EPs, Compilations and the rest are Plex's, with or without these.

- **Skip live tracks and demos** (in Settings › Playback, for everyone): with these, MusicBrainz's kind for an album Plex hasn't one for counts too.
- **Missing albums on artist pages**: **Not in your library**, under an artist's albums, lists their release groups the library doesn't have, by kind, each linked to MusicBrainz, to copy or download (CSV). Which kinds are listed is yours to choose (**Kinds…**, or Settings › MusicBrainz): albums, singles & EPs, compilations, soundtracks and remixes to begin with, without live albums, demos, DJ mixes, mixtapes and the rest.
- **Original release years**: an artist's albums are in the order they first came out, with that year (Plex often has a remaster's or reissue's year), and an album's page says when it first came out where that's earlier.
- **Artists of the same name**: Plex can have two artists of one name as one, with both of their albums on its page. Where MusicBrainz knows them apart, the other's are kept apart, under **Another artist called …** at the foot of the page (with what MusicBrainz says to tell them apart: "Brazilian rapper", say), and left out of the page's Play, Shuffle and Popular tracks. Only on plain evidence: MusicBrainz has who the page's artist is (by the id Plex has for them, where MusicBrainz knows them by it, or by more of the page's albums being theirs), and has the album for the other artist alone, by its very title and within a year of the year Plex has, and nothing like it for the page's artist. Never a self-titled album (artists of one name have those by construction), and nothing for artists with more release groups than MusicBrainz is asked for (500). Anything less, and an album stays where Plex has it. (So an album of the artist's that MusicBrainz hasn't yet, with the very title and year of one of the other's, would be kept apart: turn this off for you in Settings › MusicBrainz, or add it on MusicBrainz.) Up to three others of the name are looked at, once a month.
- **Other names**: an artist who isn't found by their name, importing a playlist (from Spotify or a file) or checking Missing tracks again, is looked for by the other names MusicBrainz has for them: other spellings and scripts ("Мумий Тролль" and "Mumiy Troll"), what they were called before. (While MusicBrainz isn't answering, other names aren't looked for, for a few minutes, so an import doesn't wait on it for each artist.)
- **For the server's owner: years and genres to fix** (Settings › MusicBrainz › Years and genres to fix): your albums whose year in Plex isn't the year they first came out, and those without a genre that MusicBrainz has genres for. **Use 1977** (or **Fix all**) and **Add** (or **Add all**) change them in Plex, for everyone and for every Plex app, locked so Plex's next refresh leaves them, with Undo just after, for one or for all of them (it puts back what was there and unlocks it; on an album in Plex, Unlock and Refresh Metadata puts back what Plex had too). Genres go on as your library names them ("Hip-Hop", not a "Hip Hop" beside it). An artist's albums of one title (a self-titled album, say: Weezer has six) are told apart by the year Plex has for each, and one that can't be is left alone; and a year MusicBrainz has as later than Plex's is shown, to check, but not fixed with the rest. Then decade browsing, Time travel radio, the Era DJ and genre browsing go by them too. Plex never writes to your files, so the list downloads too (CSV), with each album's MusicBrainz release group, for [MusicBrainz Picard](https://picard.musicbrainz.org) or [beets](https://beets.io) to fix the files' tags. The player looks the whole library up in the background, carrying on if you leave the page.
- **For the server's owner: artists to merge** (Settings › MusicBrainz › Artists to merge): artists Plex has more than once, merged in Plex, for everyone and every Plex app, as many at once as you choose, with one OK (Plex's own merge: the others' albums and tracks go to one, and the others are gone, which the player can't undo). Two kinds: artists of the very same name (Plex makes two of one from files tagged otherwise), and those whose name is several artists' in one ("Alphabeat;FORCE": the files' album artist tag has several artists joined by `;`, which Plex takes for one name), merged into the artist of the first of those names the library has. Chosen to merge to begin with only on plain evidence, album by album: MusicBrainz has albums of each of them as that artist's, by their very titles and within a year of the years Plex has (two at least, or one of a title few other artists have: not one of a single word, nor "Live at …", nor one of stock words alone, such as "Greatest Hits", "The Ultimate Collection", "BBC Sessions" or "Second Album"), and none as another's of the name. That holds even where Plex has the same MusicBrainz id for them, as Plex can take an artist MusicBrainz doesn't know for the one of that name it does. For a name of several in one: the library's artist of its first name is MusicBrainz's, plainly, by their albums, and its own albums are that artist's too, unless MusicBrainz knows only one artist of the name. Never by a self-titled album, nor for artists with more release groups than MusicBrainz is asked for, nor where MusicBrainz has more artists of the name than are looked through. What MusicBrainz has as different artists of one name, or can't tell apart, is listed as left as it is, saying why, album by album (how many albums each has, and how many MusicBrainz has as theirs): yours to choose too, the OK saying how many of those you've chosen. A name of several in one waits while Plex has the artist of its first name more than once: merge those first, and it's looked at straight away. After merging, the page shows what's left as it is then, without reading your library again (**Look again** does). Only the names Plex has more than once are looked up, several at a time on a MusicBrainz of your own. Titles as stores give them ("Shadows - EP", "Heat - Single") match MusicBrainz's, here and for the other MusicBrainz features. Plex goes by your files' album artist tags, so new files tagged as those were come in apart again: Picard or beets can tag them as one.

## Settings in the app

**They follow your Plex account**: any browser you sign in on with the same player has them, the Home screen too. The container keeps them for your account (in `/data`; `SYNC_SETTINGS=false` turns it off: see [configuration.md](configuration.md#container-settings)), and the change made last to each one wins (one made offline, or as a tab closed, is sent the next time the app opens there, unless that setting has been changed since elsewhere). The first time a browser has them, the settings you'd changed on it stay (and go to your other devices), and it takes your account's for the rest; once someone has signed out of a browser, what it still has never goes to the next account signed in on it. Some are always each device's own: Streaming quality (a work laptop on a slow connection, say), the route to your server, the player name, sharing playback with your devices, playback speed, Load ahead, and your own AI server (its key never leaves the browser). **Settings › Account › On all your devices** turns it off for your account: then nothing is kept for it, and each device has its own settings.

Each device keeps its copy when you sign out, but for the ones that are yours rather than the device's: anything for the AI, the player name and the Home layout go there, with your queue and lists (what's kept for your account stays, for when you sign in again). Switching user in a Plex Home clears them all (see below). **Settings › Experience** follows the layout of Plexamp's own settings:

- **Appearance**: colour from artwork, and the background (album colours, blurred artwork, or plain).
- **Player**: the rating, the volume control, the seekbar style, Up next side by side on wide screens, the album year, opening the full player when something starts, playback speed, the sleep timer, long-form controls (for tracks over 20 minutes), and quick skip buttons with times you choose.
- **Home screen**: the sections on Home, one layout for every library and server. Drag to reorder, tap one to show or hide it, or to pick its time period.
- **Grid size, font size and rating system** (a favourite tick, five stars, thumbs or off), codec information (formats and quality on album pages, on covers, in search and in the player), popular-track badges and preview buttons, the top plays list size, and **1-tap playback**.
- **Search all music libraries** and **playlists from all music libraries**, when your server has more than one music library.
- **Sync recent plays**, **desktop integration** (media keys, the tab title, keyboard shortcuts) and the **player name** Plex shows on its dashboard.

**Switching user, in a Plex Home**: where your Plex account is in a Plex Home with others, **Settings › Account** has **Switch user** beside **Sign out** (signed in with Plex, not with a server address and token), and so does choosing a server, for someone in the Home with none to choose. It lists everyone in the Home (you among them, marked), and asks for the PIN of anyone with one: a managed user, say. The PIN goes to plex.tv and nowhere else. Each of you is your own person here: switching clears everything this device keeps for whoever was signed in, as signing out does, and every setting too, the device's own included (Streaming quality, the player name, your own AI server), so none of it goes with them; theirs come from their account, as on any browser they sign in on. Their playlists, ratings and plays are their own in Plex, and so is what the container keeps for their account. They stay on the same server and library, where they can use them (with their own access to the server), or else choose one. The page loads afresh as them, at Home, and other tabs open on the player follow (anything one of them kept in the moment before it followed goes too). Back doesn't reach the last person's pages: going back to one shows Home, as it does after signing out. Nothing to set up: it's there whenever plex.tv says you're in a Plex Home with someone else.

**Settings › Playback** has the streaming quality, gapless playback, crossfades, Load ahead, Keep played tracks, loudness levelling, skipping tracks you've rated down (and live tracks and demos), and sharing playback with your devices; **Settings › Connection** has the route to your server, and how live updates reach this device (see [configuration.md](configuration.md#reaching-your-plex-server)).

## Good to know

- **Formats.** Files play as they are when the browser supports them (FLAC, MP3, AAC, Opus and WAV in current browsers; ALAC mostly in Safari). Anything else is converted by your server, and played with the browser's built-in HLS support or [hls.js](https://github.com/video-dev/hls.js), which is bundled into the image. A converted stream that fails part-way (a dropped connection, or the server stopping it) is tried again and then started again from where it got to; a track is only skipped if its stream keeps failing at the same place.
- **Sonic features** (similar tracks, Sonic path, sonically similar artists, artist radio and the sound-based Guest DJs) need sonic analysis turned on for the library in Plex.
- **Smart crossfade needs Plex's loudness analysis** (Plex's **Settings › Library › Analyze audio tracks for loudness**, which runs during the server's maintenance period). It tells the player where a track's music really ends, and where it ends quietly. Without it, Smart fades at the very end of each track. The waveform in the seek bar comes from the same analysis.
- **Crossfades on iPhone and iPad.** Safari there doesn't let pages change the volume of audio, so it can't fade: tracks change where the music ends instead.
- **When the connection drops** (a phone losing its signal, say): what's loaded already plays on (the track playing, and those loaded ahead: see **Load ahead**), and a track that can't load waits where it is for the connection, then plays from there once your server answers again: it's never skipped, nor converted instead, for want of the connection. Next and Previous still work meanwhile, to what's loaded. A track whose connection keeps dropping while your server answers otherwise (a weak signal, say) is loaded again as it is, from where it got to, a while later each time: never at another quality, as Streaming quality is yours to change. The player says so, and suggests a lower Streaming quality (a tap away), which uses less data. (A file the browser says it can't play is converted only once your server's handed it over: one that didn't come, as browsers say of one that doesn't, is a drop.) Plays and ratings are kept on the device (with your other things there: signing out clears them, saying so if any haven't been sent) and sent to Plex once the connection's back, so tracks played meanwhile count, as they would have; a rating made meanwhile shows at once (but one of a track rated again since in another Plex app or on another device stays as that one has it, a play isn't sent if Sync recent plays has been turned off since, nor sent twice: one that may have got there, its answer lost, is sent again only once Plex, asked a minute later, hasn't it as played). One that can't get there while your server answers otherwise waits a while, longer each time, the others going meanwhile; it's let go only once your server has plainly failed it five times (a rating saying so). The banner says what's happening till it's back (asking again every half a minute, and for another way to your server). Your Plex server being down or restarting behind the container (with `PLEX_SERVER_URL`) counts as the connection gone too.
- **Without the internet**: with your Plex server letting the player in without signing in, those signed in carry on while plex.tv can't be reached (you, your friends and your Plex Home, each as themselves), and with an offline password set on the container, someone new can sign in with it, as the server's owner, till plex.tv is back. See [configuration.md](configuration.md#offline-mode).
- **Memory.** Long lists keep artwork only near the screen, and pages you've moved on from are let go after a while, so the player stays light however long it's open or however big the library.
- **Not built yet**: lyrics and loudness levelling.
